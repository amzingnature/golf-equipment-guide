The gear checklist

Golf equipment, piece by piece.

You do not need the most expensive kit to get started. Understand the job each item does, then choose based on your experience, comfort, and budget.

🏌️
01 / The main tools

Golf clubs

Drivers, woods, hybrids, irons, wedges, and putters each support different shots. Begin with a balanced set you can control rather than chasing maximum distance.

Quick tip: Check club length, grip size, and how comfortably you can set up to the ball.
⚪
02 / Every shot

Golf balls

Golf balls differ in feel, construction, and performance. Beginners who lose a few balls per round may prefer affordable options while learning consistency.

Quick tip: Pick a ball you can afford to play regularly and learn how it feels around the green.
🎒
03 / Carry it all

Golf bag

A bag keeps clubs organised and accessories close at hand. Stand bags suit walking golfers; cart bags can work well when riding or using a trolley.

Quick tip: Consider weight, strap comfort, pocket layout, and how you travel to the course.
👟
04 / Stable footing

Golf shoes

Golf shoes are designed to provide traction during the swing. Look for a secure fit and comfort for the amount of walking you expect to do.

Quick tip: Try them on with golf socks and make sure your toes have room.
🧤
05 / Grip & feel

Gloves and grips

A glove can help manage grip and moisture, while correctly sized club grips can make the club feel more secure in your hands.

Quick tip: Replace a worn, slick glove before it becomes a distraction.
🧢
06 / Small essentials

Tees and accessories

Tees, ball markers, a divot tool, a towel, sunscreen, and water can make a round easier. Keep your setup useful instead of overpacked.

Quick tip: Check the course rules and weather before you leave home.
Before you buy

Build your bag around your game—not the adverts.

The best equipment is the gear that suits your current ability, body, playing conditions, and budget.

A smarter buying process

Four checks to make before checkout.

01

Be honest about your experience

New golfers usually benefit from forgiving, easy-to-use equipment. More advanced players may have specific needs based on ball flight and shot shape.

02

Get the fit right

Club length, shaft characteristics, grip size, and shoe fit can affect comfort and control. If possible, test equipment or seek a qualified fitting.

03

Set a realistic budget

Compare complete sets, individual clubs, and quality used gear. Keep money aside for balls, gloves, and other items you will replace.

04

Try before committing

When possible, test clubs and shoes in person. Check the return policy and inspect used clubs for damage, worn grips, and shaft condition.

Quick answers

Golf equipment FAQs

What golf equipment does a beginner need?

Start with a suitable set of clubs, golf balls, tees, a glove if you prefer one, comfortable golf shoes, and a bag. Add accessories as you discover what you use regularly.

Is it better to buy a complete golf club set?

A complete set can be a convenient starting point because the clubs are selected to work as a group. Compare the set's composition, build quality, fit, warranty, and price before buying.

Does golf club length matter?

Yes. Club length can influence posture, setup, and strike consistency. Height alone does not tell the whole story, so consider wrist-to-floor measurement, posture, and a professional fitting when practical.

Should beginners buy expensive golf balls?

Not necessarily. Choose balls that fit your budget and play consistently with them while you learn. Premium balls can offer particular performance characteristics, but they are not essential for every new golfer.

Can used golf equipment be a good choice?

Yes, used clubs can provide value if they are in good condition and suit your game. Inspect the clubface, shaft, grip, and hosel, and buy from a seller with a clear return policy when possible.

How often should golf equipment be replaced?

There is no single replacement schedule. Replace items when they are damaged, no longer fit, or have worn enough to affect use. Grips and gloves may need attention sooner than clubs.
